Research contribution

Reliability belongs inside the measurement process.

Instead of treating every spectral peak as a dependable heart-rate reading, Pulsar evaluates a motion-separated candidate and applies a training-derived reliability policy before reporting it.

That coupling of sensing, assessment and a crew-oriented interface is the central engineering design demonstrated here.

NASA human research context

Supporting smarter measurement in the next era of exploration.

Spaceflight places unique demands on human physiology. NASA's Human Research Program frames the need to understand astronaut health, exercise and the constraints of distant missions.

✦
Spaceflight health

Why monitoring context matters.

NASA identifies altered gravity and distance from Earth as key human-spaceflight hazards. Mission exercise and physiological measurement require careful interpretation, especially when movement affects wrist-worn sensing.

Pulsar translates that research context into a proposed workflow: assess the signal, determine whether a candidate supports cautious reporting, and document the crew member's context.

Evaluation design

Participant-held-out development

Outer folds hold out an entire participant. Within each training partition, a nested leave-one-participant-out procedure selects a reporting cutoff. The held-out participant does not contribute training labels or cutoff selection.

ECG references are used for retrospective scoring, not injected into the sensor-processing or runtime decision inputs.
